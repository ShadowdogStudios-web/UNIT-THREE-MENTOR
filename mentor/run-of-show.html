Fifteen class days, 50 minutes each. Every day has your role, a before-class checklist, the minute-by-minute plan, and space for notes and reflection.

Your release plan is a suggestion. Settle each day’s level with Tom at your weekly planning meeting and change it below. Your choices, checklists, and notes save on this device.

Day1Chapter 1: The Gilded Age: Wealth and Its CostWatch
Chapter 1: The Gilded Age: Wealth and Its CostCh 1 Assignment: Read the PhotographPlan: Ch 1 · The Gilded Age: Wealth and Its Cost

Before class

Minute by minute

MinTeacherStudents
0–5Do Now / hook: Project two images side by side: the Vanderbilt mansion and Hine’s breaker boys. Quick-write: “Both are 1890s–1910s America. What one word describes each?”Quick-write two sentences; share with a partner.
5–8Post the driving question: Was the Gilded Age a golden age — and if so, for whom? and the “I can” statement.Copy the driving question into My Notes.
8–25Lead the chapter presentation; pause on the Be-the-Historian slide and the vocabulary slide.Follow along, use the highlighter on key evidence, answer pause questions with a partner.
25–30Guided practice: Model the photo-analysis routine on the State Street crowd photo: what I see → who made it and why → how much I trust it.Try the first item with a partner and check it together.
30–45Release to the assignment: Read the Photograph (Photo analysis). Confer with small groups.Complete the assignment; use the self-check; work autosaves.
45–50Closure: return to the driving question; cold-call two students for an evidence-based answer.Exit ticket: answer the driving question in one sentence with one piece of evidence.
